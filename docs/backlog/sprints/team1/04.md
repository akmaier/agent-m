## Retrospective

**What went well.**
- The gates found two real faults before they reached `sprint/04`: a GitLab write that would not refuse a moved head, and
  a removed test that was the only check of a page's result.
- Every item began with failing tests, and every new test has a recorded counter-proof.
- A gap in the SPEC was found, drafted and accepted on the same morning, and the sprint went on.

**What did not.**
- The Scrum Master wrote item texts that asked for re-exports in old files, against
  `AN IMPLEMENTATION JOB CHANGES ONLY ITS MODULES`. Three developers met it in CI.
- The backlog commit `a2b24d3` went to `main` without a test run. Pushes to `main` start none, and every pull request of
  the sprint was red on three backlog checks until #97.
- The Scrum Master asked akmaier about a change to an architecture file instead of drafting it. The Boundary's words "no
  other participant changes … an architecture file" read as forbidding the draft.
- This session's permission system refused every merge by an agent, the Product Owner's included.
- The SSH key left the agent during the sprint.
- A sentence the Scrum Master drafted for MOD-artifact-edits did not match its own type.
- The Scrum Master acted as a switchboard. Every report and decision went through scrum-master-session, from the
  developers to the Product Owner and back, and akmaier heard of almost every step. akmaier, verbatim: "I don't think
  you are doing scrum correctly. There is no back and forth all the time in the process. Where is our scrum master who
  would coordinate this?" In Scrum the team works through its sprint, the Scrum Master removes what blocks it, and the
  stakeholders see the increment at the review.
- Every pull request of an item waited for a gate of the Product Owner (Development → Release testing). That makes a
  round trip of every merge, which Scrum does not have.

**Changes, each with where it goes.** An agent's changes are proposals only
(`AN AGENT'S RETROSPECTIVE CHANGES NO PROCESS BY ITSELF`).
- Applied on akmaier's instruction, not as a proposal of this retrospective: `docs/process.md` (`c76941f`).
  - Boundary: a gap is drafted at once as a proposal akmaier accepts.
  - Sprint: the Product Owner decides every merge, and scrum-master-session carries it out on the head commit it names.
- Proposed, a participant's instructions:
  - The scope of an item is the folders of its modules and the tests that name them. Anything else is a change between
    jobs, planned as such.
  - The Scrum Master's instructions for drafting items say so, and the developers' contract keeps it.
- Proposed, team agreement:
  - Before a document that tests read is pushed to `main` — the backlog, the order, a sprint record,
    `docs/process.md` — the tests that read it are run on it locally.
  - Network git commands go over HTTPS with gh's credential helper when the SSH agent holds no key, as in this sprint.
- Proposed, team agreement: a sprint runs as one coordinated run.
  - The Scrum Master starts the selected work and the Product Owner, the developers and the release tester work with
    each other.
  - The Scrum Master steps in only for an impediment.
  - akmaier hears of the sprint at its review, and in between only of an impediment no one but him can remove.
- Proposed, process model and Definition of Done:
  - The developers check the Definition of Done themselves, with CI, and merge their item when it holds.
  - The gate Development → Release testing is decided by that check, not by a round trip to the Product Owner.
  - The Product Owner judges the increment at the review.
- Proposed, process model:
  - `docs/process-models/scrum-wip.md` gives the Scrum Master only "read the repository".
  - This sprint, scrum-master-session wrote the test setup (#97) and one change between jobs (#101), drafted the change
    request and five corrections of module files, and carried out the merges.
  - The model's capabilities of the Scrum Master would say "write to the repository" as far as `docs/process.md` assigns
    work to it.
