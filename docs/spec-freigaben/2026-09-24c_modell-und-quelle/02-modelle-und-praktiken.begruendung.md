# Five models, some practices, and the standards where they belong

**What the book says** is in the table on the queue's index page. In short: the book presents five
process models (waterfall, V-model, reuse-oriented, and the agile frameworks Scrum and Kanban).
"Agile" is the family and its values; DevOps, prototyping, incremental delivery and the scaling
layers are practices that combine with a model. I had put all of them into one list.

**What a process model is, in Agent M's terms** — your wording: how the agents develop, in teams.
So `A PROCESS MODEL ORGANISES PEOPLE AND AGENTS` makes roles part of every model definition, with
the information whether a person or an agent may fill each one. In Scrum, for example, the Product
Owner is a person; development roles can be agents.

**Where IEC 62304 went.** It is registered like any other source (§2: kind *standard*, authority
*normative*). Its requirements carry "constrains: process" (entry 01) and, by
`A PROCESS REQUIREMENT ADDS TO THE MODEL`, add their gates and artifacts to whatever model the
product uses. The mechanism of the old `A PROFILE ADDS, IT DOES NOT REPLACE` survives unchanged. Its
subject was wrong: a standard is not a variant of a model. That is why the old rule is withdrawn,
not reworded.

**`A PRACTICE IS NOT A MODEL`** exists so that the interface never offers DevOps as an alternative to
Scrum. A person new to the subject would reasonably pick one, and the result would be a workflow
without roles.
